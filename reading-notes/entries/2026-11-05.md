# Notes on science, evaluation, and compute

## [Investigating vault particles with Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/research/investigating-vault-particles-with-claude) {#vault-particles}

Anthropic | 2026-11-04

- Vaults = hollow protein shells with an unknown biological function. Claude helped researchers reconstruct one from a bacterial virus.
- Help across the actual experiment: protein search, DNA design, purification, microscopy. Researchers still making the calls.
- One wrong protocol cost two weeks. Still unclear whether these vaults form in their natural hosts.

## [Progress on provable inference for large language models](https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference) {#provable-inference}

Anthropic | 2026-11-02

- FlashProve: cryptographic proof that a specific model produced an answer, without exposing its weights.
- Useful for checking model routing. Did I actually get the model I asked for?
- Still costs 70–95× the GPU time. Proves model identity, not answer correctness.

## [Learnings from auditing public evaluations](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/auditing-public-evaluations) {#evaluation-audit}

Prime Intellect | 2026-11-03

- Audited agent benchmarks: vague tool inputs, brittle graders, even answer leakage through web search.
- Opus 5.5: 70.83% → 92.15% after fixes to public tasks, tools, and grading. Same model; changed evaluation conditions.
- For my own evaluations: read the failures before explaining the score.

## [Largest AI data centers ranked by power capacity](https://epoch.ai/graphs/largest-ai-data-centers-by-power-capacity) {#data-center-power}

Epoch AI | updated 2026-11-05

- Epoch estimates 1,037 MW of electrical capacity for computing hardware at Anthropic–Amazon New Carlisle. One AI campus, over a gigawatt.
- Electrical capacity ≠ actual electricity use ≠ active training compute. Easy to mix these up.
- Scaling also means getting power, cooling, and construction done. More chips alone won’t solve that.

## [Agile Deliberation: How AI helps humans define the gray areas](https://research.google/blog/agile-deliberation-how-ai-helps-humans-define-the-gray-areas/) {#agile-deliberation}

Google Research | 2026-11-04

- Agile Deliberation helps people define subjective image categories: show borderline examples, collect feedback, refine the classifier’s instructions.
- Interesting bit: the human is still figuring out the label too. “Healthy food” sounds clear until you see the edge cases.
- 18 live sessions, two domains. Promising, but small; this post explains existing work.

## [Is the arXiv Apocalypse upon us? A break in preprint growth coincident with agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34449) {#arxiv-submission-growth}

Arxiv | 2026-10-25

- An analysis of arXiv submissions finds non-CS papers running about 17% above historical trend since December 2025.
- Solo-authored papers up about 70% in mathematics and condensed matter over a year. More papers—but how much useful new science?
- Timing lines up with agentic AI. Submission counts alone can’t establish causation or tell us about paper quality.
